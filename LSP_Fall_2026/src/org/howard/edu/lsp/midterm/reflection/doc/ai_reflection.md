AI Reflection:

1. The use of AI in my software development this semester was a vital and helpful resource. In assignment 2, I struggled to fully grasp how to obtain classes, superclasses and other details from a requirements section. I was overwhelmed and asked AI "Explain this to me like i'm 13" and " Give me a trick to remember what should be a class and what shouldn't". Claude gave me a short list of questions to ask about the requirements section that indicated whether I should make those nouns classes or not. I've used this trick with several concepts throughout my Computer Science coursework. Especially when learning new programming languages like Java and C++ because I don't have time for coding bootcamps and i've learned Python. Once you have a grasp of one coding language, new languages tend to show similarities with different syntax. 

2. Challenges: AI often hallucinates and/or uses past conversations in newer prompts. I exclusively use ClaudeAI, after ChatGPT's questionable ethics, and unless archived/deleted, past conversations are used for context. This is helpful at times, and could easily be remedied by deleting chats between use. An example of this: if I don't want AI to completely give a coding excerpt, and instead explain, it would sometimes struggle to change gears. 

3. Moving Forward: I do intend to continue using AI in my software development. Specifically, in my debugging process and in any cases where creating classes would be redundant. For example, in creating a part-time and full-time class, it saved time to input my employee class and then give Claude the parameters necessary for the other two. Also, although Eclipse, Idle and other IDE's have debugging tools, AI is easier in fixing simple syntax and logic errors.
